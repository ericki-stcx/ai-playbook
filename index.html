How Ericki works with AI in design

Two ways to bring AI into the design process: strengthen the process you already trust, or build a faster process around making, learning and iterating.

Ericki · StrategyCX · A working guide for the design team

00 · The short version

Choices create design with intent

Choose the approach that fits the project and the engagement sold to the client. In either workflow, AI helps execute and explore; the designer remains responsible for understanding the problem, making decisions and standing behind the result.

01

Keep the problem human-led

Understand the context and the problem before asking AI to generate solutions.

02

Choose the workflow for the project

AI-enhanced work keeps a familiar design method. AI-first work changes the rhythm and brings making forward.

03

Direct the AI with intent

Give AI a purposeful role and clear direction. Do not outsource the answer to it.

04

Stay accountable for the whole result

Refine what AI produces. Understand the deliverable and the reasoning behind every choice.

01 · The approaches

Choose the process, then choose where AI helps.

The difference is not simply which tool is used. It is whether AI supports an established process or the process itself is reorganized around rapid building and iteration.

AI-enhanced workflow

Strengthen the classic process

Double Diamond · Bruno Munari

Structure the project around a design methodology, then use AI to accelerate, simplify or improve individual stages and tools.

  • The design process remains the backbone.
  • AI supports selected activities, such as research, synthesis or ideation.
  • The designer frames the problem and evaluates the options.
  • Refinement and handoff can remain largely manual.
Example · Domino’s
AI-first workflow

Build to think

Make · learn · iterate

Reorganize the process to move quickly from an idea to something tangible. Use prototypes and code to think, test and keep the project moving.

  • Build-to-think replaces a slower think-to-build rhythm.
  • Produce quickly, learn from what fails, and iterate.
  • Design and code can develop together.
  • Close collaboration with developers helps maintain code quality and architecture.
Example · Trupanion
“I want AI to generate responses under my direction, not find the answers for me.”

02 · Case studies

Two projects, two roles for AI.

These examples come from Ericki’s previous role. They illustrate different ways of working rather than a single workflow to apply everywhere.

AI-first · Pet insurance

Trupanion

Trupanion is a major pet insurance provider in the United States. After the client approved the concept, Ericki built a complete design system in both Figma and code, then developed the product flows while keeping the two representations aligned.

Explore the concept in Claude Code and Figma in parallel
→
Get client approval
→
Build the design system in Figma and code
→
Develop flows with parity maintained

How parity worked: Ericki created skills that replicated code changes 1:1 in the corresponding Figma components. Figma documentation and Markdown files documented the design and code sides. The aim was to keep an up-to-date source of truth.

Team relationship: Experienced developers helped guide code quality and architecture.

AI-enhanced · Food and delivery

Domino’s

Ericki worked on developing complete features for Domino’s. AI was used mainly during ideation and visual exploration—not to define the problem.

Understand the problem independently
→
Use Claude Code to explore solution alternatives in Figma
→
Select and refine options manually
→
Handle handoff manually

How decisions were made: Ericki selected options using product knowledge and design practice, alongside feedback from other designers and stakeholders—and his own judgment as the decision-maker.

04 · Principles

Intentionality keeps the designer in control.

AI output is a starting point. The designer needs to make the purpose and rationale behind the final work their own.

Understand the context and problem before generating solutions.
Give AI a clear, intentional direction.
Use product knowledge, design judgment and feedback to choose among alternatives.
Refine AI output; do not accept it without criteria.
Make sure every element has a reason to be there.
Understand 100% of what you deliver: the artifact and the rationale behind it.
“Everything I deliver needs a reason and an explanation. I need to understand the work and own the rationale behind it.”

Ericki’s quality bar

05 · A simple decision guide

Which workflow fits this project?

Start with the project’s commitments, then consider the team setup and the kind of learning the work needs.

Consider AI-enhanced when…

The project is organized around a familiar design process, and AI can improve selected activities without changing the overall delivery model.

Consider AI-first when…

The engagement supports rapid prototyping and iteration, and designers can work closely with developers as design and code evolve together.
